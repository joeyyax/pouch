Leaving Pouch

Your domain sends through your own provider. Leaving means changing where your code sends, not rebuilding how you send.

Your domain doesn't need Pouch

A domain sends through your provider: your Amazon SES region, your credentials, your reputation. Pouch asks the provider for its DNS records and tells you to publish them. None of your sending records point at Pouch, so the domain keeps working whatever happens to Pouch.

Your code is what depends on Pouch. Anything calling POST /v1/emails stops sending when Pouch stops answering, until you point it elsewhere.

How to leave

  1. Export your data. From the messages screen, export the log as CSV or NDJSON with nested events, or bundle bodies and attachments as .eml files. POST /v1/exports does the same without a browser. Suppressions export as CSV.
  2. Point your code at your provider's SDK, or another service. You already have the provider and the credentials Pouch has been using.
  3. Revoke Pouch's credentials. Remove the access keys you gave Pouch from your provider's console.

The sending records stay as they are. They were never Pouch's to remove. If you turned on DMARC reports, change the rua address in your DMARC record, since it points at your install.

What you lose

  • The unified log. Your provider keeps far less history, and none of it spans providers.
  • The cross-domain suppression list. A bounce on one domain no longer protects the others.
  • The dashboard. No single view of every message and why it's in its state.
  • Bodies past their retention window. Pouch encrypts each body with its own key and discards the key when retention ends (45 days by default). Pouch can't open those bodies again, though a database backup from before then still holds the key.
  • Links in mail you've already sent. Unsubscribe links point at your install, and so do confirm links unless a domain set its own. They stop working when the install does.

The one real cost of staying

One endpoint and one request shape. The SDK, @joeyyax/pouch, is a single file with no dependencies, so reading it tells you everything the API does.
